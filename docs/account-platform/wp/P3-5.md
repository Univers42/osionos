## Why

  `src/store/settings/useWorkspaceSettingsStore.ts:90` (GET), `:116` (PATCH), `:135` (PATCH, the

  `scripts/bridge-collab.mjs:78-100`), called from the General panel's autosave

## Contract

### P3-5-a — one org settings route
**Security edge.** Who may call what: any **member** of the workspace may READ the settings (the
name and the icon are shown to every member in the sidebar); only the workspace **owner** or a
member whose row says `admin` may WRITE them — the same gate the invite route uses
(`scripts/bridge-workspace-invites.mjs:76-91`). What a non-owner sees: the settings object, and
403 on every write — never a partial write, never a silent no-op. What is never in an answer: the
workspace's `slug`, `source`, `owner_id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and nothing outside the
allowlist below.

1. **One new bridge module** `scripts/bridge-workspace-settings.mjs`, mounted through
   `scripts/bridge-account-mounts.mjs` — the one seam (`:30-36`); **`bridge-api.mjs` is not
   touched**. The module is appended to the `modules` list and COPYed into
   `deploy/bridge-fly/Dockerfile:24-33`. Node built-ins only, tabs, ≤ 300 lines.
2. **`GET /api/workspaces/:id/settings`** — any member.
   - Read gate (C1): `workspace.owner_id === session.userId || workspaceMembership(config, fetchImpl, session.userId, workspaceId) !== null`.
     `workspaceMembership` (`bridge-social-core.mjs:153-160`) reads only `osionos_workspace_members`, and an owner need not
     have a member row — that is why `requireSpaceMembership` (`bridge-collab.mjs:250-257`) checks `owner_id` first, and it is
     **not** exported. Reuse `workspaceMembership`; do not add a second gate.
   - One read of `osionos_workspaces` (`id, name, settings, visibility`); the settings come from
     the `settings` jsonb column (root `models/osionos-bridge-migration.sql:34`), the name from
     the `name` column (`:31`), and `visibility` from
     `models/osionos-social-migration.sql:141-143` (it is **not** in the bridge migration's
     `osionos_workspaces`, `:28-37`).
   - Answers exactly six camelCase keys: `name`, `icon`, `landingPageId`, `sidebar`, `analytics`,
     `visibility`. A jsonb key that is absent comes back as the client's default — `icon: '🌏'`,
     `landingPageId: null`, `sidebar: {newSidebar:true,showApps:true}`,

### P3-5-b — delete, leave, transfer ownership
**Security edge.**
- **Delete (staged, decision 2):** the workspace **owner only** (`osionos_workspaces.owner_id`),
  **and** a sudo grant on the current session (`requireSudo`, `scripts/bridge-guard.mjs:194-200`). A
  member with role `admin` may **not** delete the workspace. `DELETE /api/workspaces/:id` does **not**
  cascade immediately: it marks the workspace `pending_delete` with a 14-day deadline; a
  `POST /api/workspaces/:id/cancel-delete` (owner + sudo) clears the flag inside the window. The
  row, pages, memberships and share rules go on a later sweep (cascade in root
  `models/osionos-bridge-migration.sql:51,40` and `models/osionos-share-rules-migration.sql:18`). The
  server does not touch sessions.
- **Leave:** any member except the owner may leave themselves; the owner's row is immutable
  (409 `is_owner`) — the owner transfers ownership first.
- **Transfer ownership:** the **owner only**, plus sudo. The target must already be a member; the
  target becomes `owner`, the old owner becomes `admin`.
- **Who may see what (C12):** delete → owner 200 (with sudo) / admin 403 / member 403 / stranger 404;
  transfer → owner 200 (with sudo) / others 403; leave → any member 200 / owner 409 / non-member 404.
  A plain member never sees another workspace's ids.
- What a non-owner sees everywhere: 403 `forbidden`, never the ids of another workspace's rows.

1. **One new bridge module** `scripts/bridge-workspace-lifecycle.mjs`, mounted through
   `scripts/bridge-account-mounts.mjs` — the account handler already receives `kv`
   (`scripts/bridge-api.mjs:4428`), so `requireSudo` is available; **`bridge-api.mjs` is not
   touched**.
2. **`DELETE /api/workspaces/:id`** — owner + sudo (staged, decision 2).
   - Without sudo → 403 `{code:'sudo_required'}` (`scripts/bridge-guard.mjs:197`).
   - With sudo → 200 `{ok:true, pendingDelete:true, workspaceId, deleteAfter}`; the workspace row is
     marked `pending_delete` with a 14-day deadline; pages, memberships and share rules are not
     removed yet (they go on the sweep). A `POST /api/workspaces/:id/cancel-delete` (owner +
     sudo) clears the flag inside the window and answers 200 `{ok:true, cancelled:true}`.
   - What goes on the sweep vs what is orphaned (C8):
     | goes | orphaned / stays |
     |---|---|
     | `osionos_workspaces` row, `osionos_pages` (cascade), `osionos_workspace_members` (cascade), `osionos_workspace_invites` (cascade), `osionos_teams`/`osionos_team_members` (cascade), share rules whose target is the workspace or a team of it | share rules naming a **person** (`user:<id>`) survive; other devices' `activeWorkspaceByUser` is untouched (the server does not touch sessions); chat channels are not touched by this route |
   - A non-owner (including an `admin` member) → 403 `forbidden`; a stranger → 404
     `workspace_not_found` (the same answer as `scripts/bridge-collab.mjs:72`).
   - An audit row is written **before** the delete (`writeAudit`,
     `scripts/bridge-guard.mjs:224-`), event `workspace.deleted`, carrying the workspace id and
     the number of pages it took — never a token.
   - Rate limit: 5 per hour per user (`rateLimit(kv, ['workspace-delete', uid], …)`, the helper at
     `scripts/bridge-guard.mjs:167-178`).
   - Not reachable until P1-2b merges and the grace decision is taken (C10): until then every
     delete answers 403 `sudo_required` — the intended fail-closed state, stated in the contract.
     The report shows the 403 path.
   - The audit-before-delete is testable (C11): `writeAudit` uses `pg.asService()`
     (`bridge-guard.mjs:241`) and `createPg` builds it over the injected `fetchImpl`
     (`bridge-pg.mjs:152-163`), so the shared mock records the audit write in the same `calls` array
     as the delete. Test: the audit write's index in `mock.calls` is lower; details carry the
     workspace id and the page count, never a token.
3. **`POST /api/workspaces/:id/leave`** — the caller leaves.
   - 200 `{ok:true, left:true}`; the caller's `osionos_workspace_members` row is deleted.
   - The owner → 409 `is_owner` with the message "Transfer ownership before you leave." (the same
     code the member routes use, `scripts/bridge-workspace-invites.mjs:252-254`).
   - A non-member → 404 `not_a_member`.
   - The publish `op:'removed'` to `user:<caller>` is reused
     (`scripts/bridge-workspace-invites.mjs:267-273`).
   - What it removes (C9): the caller's `osionos_workspace_members` row (team FK cascades drop them
     from every team); share rules naming the leaver are removed; pending invite rows addressed to
     them are revoked. The workspace must keep exactly one owner row: the owner of a one-member
     workspace cannot leave (409 `is_owner`) — transfer ownership first.
4. **`POST /api/workspaces/:id/members/transfer-ownership`** — owner + sudo. This is the route the
   store already calls (`src/store/settings/useWorkspaceMembersStore.ts:105`).
   - Body `{toUserId}` — a uuid; a target that is not a member → 404 `member_not_found`.
   - Without sudo → 403 `sudo_required`; a non-owner → 403 `forbidden`.
   - With sudo: the target's row becomes `role:'owner'` with the owner's permission set
     (`['create','read','update','delete','admin']`), the old owner's row becomes `role:'admin'`.
   - **Atomicity:** the two rows change in **one** transaction. PostgREST cannot write two rows of
     two tables in one transaction, so the write goes through one SQL function,
     `public.osionos_workspace_transfer_ownership(p_workspace uuid, p_from uuid, p_to uuid)`,
     added by the root file of this package (`models/osionos-workspace-lifecycle-migration.sql`).
     The function runs as `service_role` only: `REVOKE EXECUTE ... FROM PUBLIC, anon, authenticated`,
     `GRANT EXECUTE ... TO service_role`; `search_path` pinned; `lock_timeout` set; both member rows
     are locked `FOR UPDATE` in `ORDER BY user_id` to avoid a deadlock on concurrent A->B / B->A
     transfers (C7). The state must never be "two owners" or "no owner"; a test asserts it.
   - The client store already moves the roles optimistically (`transferOwnerRole`,
     `src/store/settings/useWorkspaceMembersStore.ts:41-45`); the server's answer replaces the
     roster.
5. **The client, in place.**
   - `src/features/auth/model/useUserStore.ts:600-612` — `deleteWorkspace` **stops swallowing the
     error**: it returns `false` and carries the failure, so the panel can tell the truth. The
     success toast moves out of the store and into the panel.
   - `src/features/settings/general/GeneralPanel.tsx` — the danger zone holds **both** lifecycle
     actions: "Delete workspace" (owner only) and "Leave workspace" (every other member). Both go
     through `runAccountAction` (`src/features/settings/model/settingsCalls.ts:54-74`) and show
     `accountActionNotice` (`:81-88`); a `sudo_required` outcome says nothing extra, because the
     shared fetch path raises the prompt (`src/shared/api/sudoRequired.ts:63-77`). The typed
     confirm (`:136`) stays; the sudo step is P1-6's prompt component — **until that prompt
     exists, the panel shows the 403's message** and the report says so.
    - `src/features/settings/people/PeoplePeek.tsx:92` — "Transfer ownership" is rendered **only**
      for the workspace owner. The peek's props carry no `activeUserId`
      (`PeoplePeek.tsx:28-33`); resolve the caller via `useUserStore(activeUserId)` and compare to
      the workspace's `owner_id`. Do **not** read the members store's row — it fabricates a local
      `owner` row (`useWorkspaceMembersStore.ts:35-39`). The shared typed confirm replaces the
      arm-once `ConfirmButton` (`:37-47`).

### P3-5-c — pending email invites (token hash, expiry, accept, resend, revoke)
**Security edge.**
- Create / list / resend / revoke: the workspace **owner** or an `admin` member (the existing
  gate, `scripts/bridge-workspace-invites.mjs:76-91`). A plain member → 403, a stranger → 404 —
  never an empty list, so the pending list is no oracle.
- Who may grant which role (C15): only the owner may invite an `admin`; an `admin` may invite
  `member`/`guest` only. An admin inviting an admin → 403. Test it.
- Accept: the holder of the token, **signed in**. The membership is written for the **session's
  user**, and the session's user must be the account the invite was addressed to — the invite row
  stores the resolved `invited_user_id`, so the check is an id comparison, not an email one. A
  different account → 403 `invite_wrong_account`. A stolen token must not hand a third party a
  membership.
- The token is stored **hashed**; the plaintext exists once, in the create/resend answer, and is
  never persisted by the client.

1. **Root SQL** — new `models/osionos-workspace-invites-migration.sql`:
   - `public.osionos_workspace_invites`:
     `id uuid PK`, `workspace_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES public.osionos_workspaces(id) ON DELETE
     CASCADE`, `email text NOT NULL` (display only), `invited_user_id uuid NOT NULL`,
     `role text NOT NULL CHECK (role IN ('admin','editor','viewer'))` — the **DB** roles the app
     writes (`scripts/bridge-workspace-invites.mjs:46-50`), never `owner`,
     `token_hash text NOT NULL` (the sha256 hex; the plaintext is never stored),
     `expires_at timestamptz NOT NULL`, `accepted_at timestamptz NULL`, `accepted_by uuid NULL`,
     `revoked_at timestamptz NULL`, `invited_by uuid NOT NULL`, `created_at`, `updated_at`;
     a **partial unique index** on `(workspace_id, invited_user_id)` `WHERE accepted_at IS NULL AND
     revoked_at IS NULL` — one live invite per account per workspace. The write path does **not**
     use an upsert against this index (C13): PostgREST's `on_conflict=workspace_id,invited_user_id`
     emits `ON CONFLICT (cols) DO UPDATE` and Postgres will not infer a *partial* index (42P10).

     index is the concurrency backstop. Re-inviting after acceptance is allowed (a new pending row).

### P3-5-d — teams (`osionos_teams` / `osionos_team_members`, a team page, add members)
**Security edge.** Who may call what: any **member** of the workspace may read the teams and their

## Decisions (D-P3-5s)

1. **Mail leg (Q1)** — **No transport in this package; P3-5-c ships the copyable accept link and names
   the mail service as a separate package.** One reason: there is no SMTP client, no mail route and no
   bounce policy anywhere, so a half-built transport is a second, worse path to a secret-bearing link.
   Decide the *service* before the mail package, not before P3-5-c.

2. **Delete grace (Q2)** — **No grace, but stage the delete: `DELETE /api/workspaces/:id` marks the
   workspace `pending_delete` with a 14-day deadline and a `POST …/cancel`; the row and the pages go
   on a later sweep.** One reason: the current shape (immediate + cascade, spec:201-210) is a one-way
   door with no restore path, and the spec asks the question two lines after specifying the irreversible
   answer. If immediate is insisted on, P3-5-b must show a page count and type the **uuid**, not the
   workspace name (the name is not secret — spec:52).

3. **Sudo grant (Q3)** — **Keep P1-2b → P3-5-b → P1-6; P3-5-b ships no grant route.** One reason:
   P1-2b owns `scripts/bridge-account-sudo.mjs` and a second copy is exactly the duplication the
   user's rule forbids. P3-5-b's routes stay 403 `sudo_required` until P1-2b merges — the intended
   fail-closed state, stated in the contract.

4. **Who may see a team (Q4)** — **Workspace-scoped, visible to every member; no closed teams in this
   plan.** One reason: a private team cannot be a share target (a rule the caller cannot see is a rule
   they cannot audit) and the share picker needs the full list. A closed team later is a new visibility
   column, not a filter change.

5. **`?invite=` landing (Q5)** — **Sign-in first, then the accept; no dedicated panel.** One reason:
   the accept needs a session and the deep-link seam is one-shot, dropping itself from the URL
   (`src/app/lazyAppRegions.tsx:72-78`); a panel is a second surface for the same action. The token
   must survive the sign-in round trip.

6. **Teams section name (Q6)** — **Label "Teams", id stays `teamspaces`.** One reason: the id is
   referenced by saved links and the palette (`src/features/settings/model/sections.ts:14-16,216-219`)
   and by the registry entry (`src/features/settings/ui/sectionPanels.tsx:74`); a second section
   duplicates the panel.

7. **`no_access` for the share target (Q7)** — **Yes, a team may be denied and a deny beats every grant.**
   One reason: P3-6's decision 2 is deny-beats-grant; exempting teams would make a team the only
   principal a page owner cannot restrict.
