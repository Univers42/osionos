## Who we sell to

Northwind Atelier builds contract furniture for hospitality, healthcare, culture and
workplace clients in Scandinavia, France and the Benelux. Sixty people work here: thirty-one
in the Vejle workshop, eleven in the Copenhagen showroom, eight in the Lyon studio and ten in
sales, service and administration. We do not sell to consumers and we do not run a dealer
network. Every project is quoted, produced and installed by our own teams.

Our buyers are usually three people: an operations lead who owns the budget, an architect or
designer who owns the drawings, and a site manager who owns the programme. The operations
lead signs, the designer specifies and the site manager complains about the dates. A good
quote answers all three.

## The four stages

Deals move through four stages and we report them honestly every Monday.

- **Discovery** — we have a problem statement, a rough budget and nobody has drawn anything.
- **Proposal** — a priced offer is with the client, usually with a full drawing set.
- **Negotiation** — the client wants changes, and the commercials are being argued about.
- **Won** — signed, with a deposit invoiced.

Nothing sits in a stage for more than six weeks without a written note explaining why. A
stage is a statement about what the client has agreed to do, not about how we feel about the
probability of a signature.

## What a quote contains

A quote is not a price list with a discount on top. Every quote we send has seven parts.

- The room-by-room schedule with quantities and finishes.
- A drawing set with fixings and service clearances marked.
- The production window in working weeks, starting from the deposit date.
- The installation plan, including who lifts and who protects the floor.
- The payment milestones, which follow the company standard unless we have written approval.
- The warranty statement, five years on structure and two years on finishes.
- The exclusions list, which is never empty.

## How we lose work

Three reasons account for most losses, and all three are internal.

- We quote a lead time we cannot keep because we did not ask the workshop for capacity.
- We hide an exclusion and discover it during installation, which costs us the relationship.
- We let a discount conversation start before the scope is written down.

The workshop is the constraint, not the sales team, and a quote the workshop cannot
schedule is a quote we will miss. <!-- answer:q18 -->

### How a deal enters the forecast

An opportunity enters the forecast when there is a written request from inside the client, and it
leaves the forecast when four weeks pass without one. The stage is updated by the person who owns the
relationship, in the weekly review, with the reason in the notes. A stage that nobody can explain is a
stage that does not exist.

## Numbers we watch

We track four numbers per opportunity: gross value, weighted value, days since the last
meaningful contact, and the number of people inside the client who have asked for something
in writing. A weighted value uses stage factors of ten per cent for discovery, thirty-five
per cent for proposal, sixty per cent for negotiation and one hundred per cent once signed.
If an opportunity has no written request from inside the client for more than four weeks, it
is not live, whatever the stage says.
