Ben, the workshop foreman, the quality lead, the service technician and Chloe. Forty

## How we measure

Quality is measured on what comes back, not on what we inspected before shipping. Every
service visit is logged against a product, a batch and a reason. The workshop reads the log
every month and the top three reasons drive the agenda.

The 2026 workshop audit is filed under the reference AUDIT-77, with two observations and no
blocking findings. <!-- answer:q12 -->

## The top three reasons

- Loose fittings after six months, mostly on the wall shelf.
- Seams opening on the conference table top.
- Swelling at the end of the worktop nearest the sink in healthcare projects.

The first is a fixings problem, the second is a glue problem, and the third is a sealing
problem that the clinic specification makes worse.

## The rules we agreed

A chair that squeaks after six months is a manufacturing fault, not a care issue, and we
replace it. <!-- answer:q23,q54 -->

- Anything that moves or squeaks inside the first year is replaced, not repaired.
- A seam that opens on a table top is a batch fault, and the batch is reviewed.
- Swelling near water is covered by the two-year finish warranty.
- A service visit inside the warranty year is free, including the travel.

### What a batch fault looks like

A batch fault is not one bad piece. It is a run of pieces from the same batch of material, made in
the same week, by the same shift, and it shows up in the service log as the same reason with the same
batch number. The wall shelf fixings are the clearest example: three sites, three batches, the same
complaint, and the fix was a single concealed plate instead of two screws.

## Changes to the workshop

- Wall shelf fixings change to a single concealed plate.
- Table top seams move to a mechanical joint with an adhesive backup.
- The worktop sealer for healthcare is now a two-coat system with a cure time between coats.
