## Format

Sixty people in the Vejle canteen, plus fourteen on the link from Copenhagen and Lyon. Ninety
minutes, questions at the end, no slides beyond the three numbers.

## The three numbers

- Sixty people work here, thirty-one in the workshop, eleven in the showroom, eight in
  Lyon and ten elsewhere.
- Order intake for the first quarter was eleven per cent up on the same quarter of 2025.
- The workshop delivered four hundred and twelve pieces in March, the busiest month on
  record.

## What the Lyon studio does

The Lyon studio opened in September with three people and is now eight. It handles the
French-speaking accounts, the drawings for the Lyon showroom and, since March, the French
half of the catalogue.

Nothing about the Lyon studio is a satellite office. It quotes, it draws, it visits sites
and it signs for France and Belgium. The studio lead reports to the workshop foreman on
capacity and to Ana on commercials. <!-- answer:q26 -->

## What changes for people

- From June, everyone gets two days a year of paid volunteering, booked like leave.
- The canteen moves to a later start during the summer months, from 7:30 to 8:00.
- Everyone in the workshop gets a hearing check every second year, paid by the company.
- The catalogue in French is printed once a year, in March, and not on demand.

### What the numbers mean for the floor

Four hundred and twelve pieces in March means the workshop ran three shifts for part of the month,
and it means eleven weeks of sanding backlog at the end of it. The canteen start time moved because
people arrive earlier in the summer, not because the day got shorter. The hearing check every second
year is paid by the company because the sanding line is the noisy one and the noise is ours.

## Questions from the floor

Someone asked what happens to the catalogue when a product leaves the range. The answer is
that the catalogue keeps the last drawing and the spare parts list for ten years, and that
the drawing set is available to anyone who asks.

Someone asked why the sanding backlog is not simply hired away. The answer is that the
bottleneck is extraction, not hands, and the extraction is being rebuilt.
