## Annual leave

Everyone has twenty-eight days of paid annual leave a year, plus the public holidays between
Christmas and New Year, which are not counted. Nobody is expected to take more than
twenty-eight days and nobody is asked to explain why they took fewer.

Annual leave must be booked in the people system before the first day of the requested
month, and no more than twenty-five consecutive days may be taken. <!-- answer:q15,q52 -->

## Booking rules

- A request is confirmed by the team leader within five working days.
- A booking covering more than ten days is confirmed by the people team as well.
- The workshop closes for two weeks in July and two weeks in December. The leave taken in
  those weeks is the leave taken first.
- Public holidays are not deducted from the twenty-eight days.

## Sick leave

Sick leave is paid in full for the first fourteen days and at half rate for the next
fourteen. It is booked the same morning, by message, with no certificate below three days
and a certificate from day four.

Unplanned absence is not annual leave and is handled by the team leader the same morning.

## Special leave

- Care for a sick child: up to ten days a year, no reason required.
- A family event: three days, used once.
- Study leave: five days a year for a course the team leader agrees to.
- Unpaid leave: by arrangement with the people team, in writing, with an end date.

### Holiday pay

Holiday pay is calculated on the last three months of paid salary, which is why a month without a
complete month of work lowers it. The rule is the same for everybody and it is explained in the
contract, not in a note on the intranet. Nobody has to ask for it, and nobody is asked to prove that
they took their break.

## Carry-over

Up to five unused days may be carried into the following year and must be taken before the
end of March. Beyond five days, the days are lost, and nobody is asked to justify a lost
day.

## Sick leave and the handover rule

A person who is away for more than three consecutive days hands over their open work
before they go. This is not about trust. It is about the workshop, which cannot wait for a
message.
