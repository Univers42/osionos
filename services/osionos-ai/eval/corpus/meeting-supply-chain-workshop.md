## Why we met

The paint line is the constraint on everything. Sanding is backed up eleven weeks, assembly
four, and the paint line is booked solid until the middle of September. Twenty people spent
a morning looking at the flow from goods-in to dispatch.

## The three findings

- Goods-in and the finishing cell compete for the same aisle, so half-finished work sits in
  the traffic.
- The extraction filter on the paint line was replaced twice in a year, which means the
  filter specification is wrong for the varnish we use.
- Two products, the wall shelf and the small side table, wait eleven days for a finishing
  step that takes four hours.

## The decision

The workshop meeting decided to rebuild the paint line and the finishing cell before the
museum installation, moving the goods-in aisle and giving the finishing cell its own
staging area. <!-- answer:q32 -->

The reorganisation of the paint line and the finishing cell, codenamed ATELIER-BRIDGE,
starts in week 34 and runs until the end of January. <!-- answer:q09 -->

## What it costs and what it buys

- The rebuild costs eleven weeks of capacity in the workshop and nothing in cash beyond the
  filter and the aisle.
- It removes eleven weeks of sanding backlog if the extraction is right the first time.
- It cannot start before the museum installation, because the museum takes the workshop.

## Stock and spare parts

The same morning produced a stock decision. We keep sheet goods for the three most-used
products and a small box of fittings for every catalogue piece, and everything else is
ordered against the programme. The parts list is now written down in the runbook, with the
lead time next to each line. <!-- answer:q32 -->

### What we measured

The morning counted the pieces that moved twice. Half of the sanding backlog was not sanding, it was
work sitting in the aisle between the two cells, waiting for a free staging place. The second number
was the wall shelf: eleven days of waiting for a finishing step that takes four hours, which is the whole
argument for moving the goods-in aisle and giving the finishing cell its own area.
