## The ladder

Discounts are not negotiated freely, they follow a ladder with named steps. Every request is
measured against the list price of the moment, and every step needs a named approver.

- Up to five per cent — the account manager may agree it alone.
- Six to twelve per cent — the sales director signs it.
- Above twelve per cent — the sales director and the workshop manager sign it together.

Nothing above twelve per cent has been approved on a call. If a client wants more, the
answer is a scope change, not a bigger discount.

## What the discount may not cover

- Installation and freight, which are costed separately and never discounted.
- The custom reception and counter pieces, which are quoted from a drawing.
- The shelving and storage family, where the margin is already thin.
- Any item that carries a surcharge for a finish or a non-standard length.

Every piece finished in the NW-FERROLLE smoked oak line carries a six per cent surcharge
over the standard oak price list. <!-- answer:q11 -->

## Surcharges that are not discounts

Surcharges are listed on the quote as their own line, in the same place as the freight. They
are not negotiable, because they price a real extra cost: a different timber batch, a longer
production time, a harder surface treatment.

- Non-standard lengths, over two metres, are priced per piece.
- Smoked and fumed finishes carry the surcharge above.
- Any timber that is not in the standard list is priced by the board, on the day.
- Integrated power and cable management are priced per module.

## How a discount request is answered

The answer to a request is always the same shape: the discount we can give, the scope
change that gets the rest of the value, and the date the answer expires. A request for nine
per cent on a room programme is answered with two per cent and a revised programme, not with
a negotiation about whether eight per cent is possible. <!-- answer:q19 -->

A project of four hundred thousand euros and above is quoted with the discount ladder
attached, so nobody has to remember the steps. <!-- answer:q30 -->

### Who approves what

Two signatures are needed above twelve per cent and both of them are in the workshop, not in the
sales team. The workshop manager signs because the discount is paid from the material margin, and
that margin is set by what the workshop can buy and how quickly it can buy it. A discount agreed
without that signature is a discount the workshop pays for.

## Rules that are not negotiable

- The five-year structural warranty is in every contract and is never traded away.
- The thirty, sixty, ten payment schedule is the default and only changes in writing.
- Spare parts availability for ten years is a contractual promise, not a courtesy.
- Nothing goes out of the standard range without the workshop manager's written approval.

## Where the money actually is

The discount is rarely the reason a deal fails. Scope confusion is. A client who asked for
nine per cent usually wanted a cheaper programme, and the programme was wrong. Reopening
the schedule costs a week and often wins the work.
