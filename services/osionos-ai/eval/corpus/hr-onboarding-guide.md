## Before the first day

The people team sends a short welcome pack on the Thursday before a start date. It contains
the building map, the wifi instructions, the first week's plan and the name of the buddy.
Nothing in it is long, because nobody reads anything long in their first week.

A start date is only confirmed once the following three things are true.

- The contract is signed and the payroll record exists.
- The team leader has named a buddy and told the buddy.
- The workstation is ready, with a screen, a lamp and a drawer that locks.

## The first week

Every new joiner spends their first week with a buddy from the workshop and keeps a
training log signed off by their team lead. <!-- answer:q13 -->

The buddy is not a mentor and not a teacher. The buddy shows where things are, how the
machine works and where the coffee is. The training log is a list of tasks with a date and
a signature, and it is the only thing that proves the first week happened.

## The first month

- Week two: the person works on a real job with the team, at whatever level the team lead
  chooses.
- Week three: a safety induction in the workshop, including the extraction rules and the
  hearing protection.
- Week four: a half-hour conversation with the people team about the contract, the pay
  structure and anything unclear.

## After the first month

Nobody finishes an onboarding. The guide continues in two parts: a check at three months
with the team leader, and a check at six months with the people team and the team leader
together. Both checks are short, written down, and kept with the contract.

### What the buddy does not do

The buddy does not check the work, does not assess the person and does not report on the first
week to the team leader. The buddy answers questions, shows where things are and says out loud when
something is not obvious. A team leader who wants to know how the first week went reads the training
log and asks the person.

## What we do not do

- We do not give new joiners a laptop on their first day without a configuration check.
- We do not schedule project work in the first two weeks of a workshop role.
- We do not let a team leader run an onboarding without a buddy, even in a small team.
- We do not treat the training log as paperwork to be filed; the team lead reads it.

## The number we watch

Thirty per cent of people who leave in their first year had no completed training log. It is
the clearest early warning we have, and it costs nothing to fix.
